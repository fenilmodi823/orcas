ORCAS · Phase 3 · deliverable 5

Logo candidates

Three treatments of the Radar Wireframe direction already chosen in Branding.md — an orca whose body is built from orbital tracks, radar rings fading off the nose. All three share one master silhouette, so whichever you pick, the geometry underneath is identical and the other two remain available as variants.

How to judge these. Look at the 24 px column first — that is the real constraint, and it is where most marks die. Then check the inverted row: the mark has to survive on a light background for the paper, a slide deck and a printed badge. Everything else is negotiable.

Candidate A — Solid

The silhouette carries the mark; the orbital tracks appear only in the animation, drawing themselves and then filling in. Strongest at small sizes, warmest, least "tech-y" at rest.

Which one, and why it matters

Candidate Strongest at Weakest at Animation
A · Solid 16–32px, favicons, avatars, print, one colour Says "orca" more than "orbital instrument" at rest Tracks draw first, then the body resolves — the storyboard works exactly as written
B · Track body Landing sequence, OG card, the paper's title slide Needs a separate small variant below 32px Best of the three — the mark is literally built from drawable paths
C · Line Over glass, on light backgrounds, at large sizes 24px, and any context where stroke weight can't scale A single continuous stroke draw — the most satisfying, the least legible small
Recommendation: A as the working mark, B as the animated and large-format lockup. They share the same silhouette, so this is one logo with two renderings, not two logos — which is what Branding.md's "monogram variant for compact contexts" requirement is already asking for. C stays in the file as the light-background variant if you ever need it.
